HOW IT WORKS
The fight

You never control a lion. You build one, or you own one, and it fights for itself. Every lion runs its own head: it holds the range its weapon wants, strafes, reads incoming shots and steps off the line, and spends its abilities when they matter. The mind you pick decides how it thinks.

Frames and weapons

Bigger frames carry slightly more health, hit harder, and swing and move noticeably slower. Smaller frames are the reverse. Across the whole size range the damage per second comes out close, so what you are really choosing is tempo. Weapons have weight too: claws let you move at 1.16×, a Sunbeam drags you down to 0.86×. Reach costs you your legs.

Duel

Best of three, no exceptions. Both lions return to full health at the start of every round. Paste a lion code into either slot to fight someone else's lion. Switch to 2v2 for two teams of two: a round ends only when both lions on one side are down, and teammates never hit each other.

Tournament

Eight lions of your choosing in a knockout bracket. Every match is best of three, winners go through, and three rounds later there is one champion. Edit, roll or paste a code into any slot before it starts. Nothing in a tournament touches your championship lion.

Career

You build your own lion: frame, armour, weapon, abilities, mind, personality, coat, mane and tail. Hit Roll if you would rather let chance decide, then tweak whatever you like. Its IV is drawn the moment you start and is not yours to touch. When you press Finish lion it is locked for good — nothing changes again until it dies or you retire it, and then you build the next one.

A championship is five bouts against five different lions. Every bout starts at full health for both of them (unless you have made a deal with the Mystic Lion), so a run is five honest fights rather than a war of attrition. Win all five and it takes the belt and a medallion.

Gold and the Lion Store

Every career lion carries its own gold. It earns 5 to 15 for each championship bout it wins, and 1 to 3 for each wave of the pack it clears. Spend it in the Lion Store in the den, where a red lion in a gold monocle sells a Colour Change (20), a Personality Potion that rerolls one trait of your choosing (30) and a new Weapon (60). Whatever you buy goes into the lion’s own Inventory, and you use it from there when you like. Beat a lion in a championship and about one time in three you take its skull home; skulls stack in the bag. The Mystic Lion takes 7 skulls for a soul: Divine (eyes white as an angel’s, a holy light) or Demonic (red eyes, hellfire). Either gives +10% to every stat, and a lion carries one soul at a time. For 3 skulls it also sells Heavenly Protection: if your lion would die in a championship, the blessing burns away instead and it lives. The Lion Store sells an Upgrade Token (100 gold) too: a guaranteed training session, stat of your choice. The Blacksmith finishes your lion’s weapon in Bronze, Obsidian and Gilded (50, 150, 250 gold), +2% damage a level, lost if the weapon changes, and sells Reforge Armour (50). The Mystic Lion also has a Soul Cleanse (2 skulls) and a Blood Pact (5 skulls, rerolls IV), and the Lion Store a Name Plaque (60 gold). Lions remember you. One that beats you and lets you live becomes a rival, and so might one you only just beat; rivals come back in later championships, a little harder each time, and keep score. A lion that kills yours becomes your nemesis: it comes back for your next lion, as the final, with red eyes and a kill for every lion it took. Beat it and it is avenged, for 40 gold and 3 skulls. Under the stands, the Hyena Fence buys skulls for 25 gold each, sells Mystery Crates (45 gold) and keeps three deals, 5 to 10 gold off, that change after every bout. When the lion dies, its gold goes with it.

Scars

Every loss your lion survives leaves a mark, half the time across the face and half the time down the body. Scars are permanent, they show on the lion wherever you see it, and they travel in its code. They cost nothing but they are a record you cannot edit.

Losing a bout

Rookie championships. Your first two championships are kinder: the opponents start out smaller than your lion and build up over the five bouts, and each run comes with one second chance (lose a bout and Old Lion patches your lion up for a rematch instead of the run ending). Before its first belt, a lion can’t die; before its second, the chance is halved. After that the climb is gradual rather than a cliff: opponents in championships 3 to 6 are still a little short of full strength, a bit less each time, and from the seventh on they are at full strength and slowly getting bigger.

When your lion loses, it has a 20% chance of dying there and then. Four times in five it gets dragged out alive, carrying the loss, and the championship run is over. The fifth time it does not get up, and it is gone permanently — its record goes to the hall of fame and you build a new lion.

Medallions

Each belt grants one random medallion, each with its own emblem: Ironheart (+9% health), Swiftpaw (+7% speed), Keen Fang (+9% damage), Scarhide (+6 armour), Quickened (−9% ability cooldowns), Ember Blood (heals 1 a second) or Loose Shoulder (−8% attack cooldown). They stack across belts.

Training

After every championship win there is a 35% chance of a training opportunity. You pick the stat: health, damage, speed, swing or armour, and the lion gains a little (2 to 3%) for good. Trained gains show as a green extension on its stat bars and on its lion card.

Events

After a championship win, with another bout still to come, there is a 20% chance of an event. The den shows it, the bottom button turns into An event awaits, and the next bout waits until you have gone out and seen it. There are six. The Royal Lion, a big grey lion in a crown and a red sash with two guards, offers a wager on your next bout: win and your gold is doubled, lose and he takes every coin. The Mystic Lion, sat by a campfire, offers two skulls if your lion starts its next bout on 75% health. The Lion Store owner wants your lion as his mascot: sign and everything in his store is 30% off for your next five bouts. Sometimes you get robbed: four hyenas under the stands want 10% of your gold. Pay up, or fight them off and keep it, with a 40% chance your lion comes away with a cut eye, a scar straight through one eye that it wears for good. Old Lion invites you into his hut: 50 gold for a lesson, and you pick the stat to train, for good. And deep in the forest sits a crate: open it for a free random item, then fight off the five hyenas that were waiting for you. Beat them and the item is yours; lose and they take it back (losing to them never kills your lion or touches its record). Say no to any of them and nothing happens.

The Bookie

A green lion in a flat cap runs fights between two other lions, and takes bets. You see both lions (record, frame, weapon, mind and size) and his odds, back one, and watch the fight. Win and you get your stake times the odds. The odds come mostly from size, and bigger usually wins. They lean very slightly your way, so over time it pays, but slowly: he runs three fights between each of your championship bouts, and the most you can stake is 25 gold, plus 15 for each belt your lion holds (up to 100). You can also just watch for free. Your lion keeps a running tally of its bets.

Loading a lion into the den

Any lion code can be loaded into Championship from the den, with its belts, medallions, scars, training and gold. If you already have a lion, it retires to the hall to make room.

Personality

Every lion is born with two personality traits out of twenty-three, and they never change. Some bend how it thinks — Bold closes and stays closed, Wary fights at arm's length, Patient reads the fight quicker. Some are physical. Some only matter in particular moments: Bloodscent finishes wounded animals, Grudge grows angrier with every scar it carries, Showman swings faster while the crowd is up, Houndsbane was built for the pack. Two touch your career directly — Lucky drops the death chance on a lost bout to 12%, Reckless raises it to 30% and hits harder for it. Opposites never appear on the same lion.

IV

Every lion is born with an individual value from 1 to 10 that never changes. It shifts health and damage by roughly two percent either way. Between neighbouring numbers it is noise. A 10 against a 1 wins about seven times in ten, all else equal — but a better loadout beats a better IV every time.

The pack

Your career lion alone against waves of dogs and hyenas, recovering only a sliver of health between them. Each wave is larger and slightly stronger. You are playing for a number: how many waves it lasts and how many it puts down. Nothing there is permanent — the lion always walks away, whatever the pack does to it.

Challenges

Four of them, and none touches your championship record. The pack throws waves of dogs and hyenas at you until you go down. The Lion King is a single fight against a titan in black plate with a great maul, far bigger than anything you will ever own. The Ancient Lion is older still: a navy-and-gold giant, half as big again as a titan, slow as a glacier, and it answers anything close with a gold nova and anything far away with falling stars. Old Lion comes out of retirement for one last fight, and he has not forgotten a thing. You can bring up to two other lions along for any of them: roll a stranger, open a saved .txt, or paste a code.

Lion codes

Every career lion has a code carrying its build, coat, IV, medallions and record. Copy it or save it as a text file, and it can be pasted into any duel slot on any device. Belts show as a gold belt on the lion and in its stats. When you load a career lion into a duel, the Editor or as an ally, you're asked whether to bring its medallions and buffs along or load it as a blank slate.
